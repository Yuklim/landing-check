## How Much Can Foreign Visitors Spend? Current Limits and Fees (2026)

As of 2026, official policy has improved access for foreigners. The single-transaction limit for overseas cards linked to Chinese mobile wallets has been raised to $5,000 USD, with an annual cumulative limit of $50,000 USD, and both platforms have simplified passport verification flows.

**Transaction Fees for Foreign Cards:**

- Transactions under RMB 200 (~$27 USD): 0% fee
- Transactions above RMB 200: approximately 3% foreign card processing fee applies
- Annual cumulative limit: $50,000 USD per calendar year
- Single transaction maximum: $5,000 USD

**Source: Alipay International Services Policy (September 2026); WeChat Pay Cross Border Payment Guidelines (2026)**

## How to Pay in China as a Tourist? All Ways Compared

China’s retail payment landscape is overwhelmingly built around **mobile QR payments**, which cover nearly all daily scenarios from street snacks to public transport across most urban areas.

For short-term international visitors, the most reliable overall strategy is to set up**Alipay** as your primary payment method, use **WeChat Pay**as a targeted backup for restaurant QR ordering and mini-program services, and keep a small reserve of RMB cash plus an international Visa or Mastercard for emergencies, remote areas and large hotel bills.

## Alipay vs WeChat Pay: Which is Better for Transport?

Both Alipay and WeChat Pay work across almost all transport scenarios in China, with minor differences in reliability:

![How to set up your metro QR code in Alipay for public transport](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/1n94a324x99kxhacw225C_Q60.jpg?proc=format/f_webp)

![Alipay vs WeChat Pay for Transport](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/1n921324x99kwdrw071DA.jpg?proc=format/f_webp)

- **Subways and buses**: Both apps offer transit QR codes via mini-programs with nearly identical city coverage. Alipay’s activation process is simpler and more straightforward for international users.
- **Ride-hailing (e.g. DiDi)**: Both platforms have functional mini-programs for booking rides. Alipay has a higher payment success rate for foreign-issued cards, while WeChat has a more integrated mini-program ecosystem.
- **Taxis and private car services**: Most drivers accept both payment methods, but some individual drivers and small operators only have WeChat QR codes available.

**Recommendation**: Use Alipay as your default transport payment method, and keep WeChat as a backup for cases where Alipay is not accepted.

## Alipay vs WeChat Pay: Which to Use for Shopping?

- **Large malls, chain stores, and scenic spots**: Almost universally accept both payment methods. Alipay delivers more consistent success with foreign cards, especially for larger purchases.
- **Alibaba ecosystem platforms (Taobao, Tmall, 1688, etc.)**: Alipay is the official and native payment channel, with full functionality and seamless integration — it is the clear and only optimal choice here.
- **Non-Alibaba e-commerce (e.g. JD.com)**: JD.com began rolling out Alipay support in late 2024, but coverage remains partial as of 2026 — many merchants and product categories still do not accept Alipay. WeChat Pay remains the more reliable mobile payment option for JD.com.
- **Small shops and street vendors**: Both QR codes are very common, but it is not unusual for a vendor to only support one. Having both apps installed ensures you will never be unable to pay.

## How to Use Alipay in China for Tourists

![How to Use Alipay in China for Tourists](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/1n933324x99kwb2rgE01F.jpg)

1. **Pre-departure setup** Download the Alipay app, select your home country/region, and register with your international mobile number. Navigate to identity verification, choose “Passport” as your ID type, upload a clear photo of your passport information page, and complete facial recognition. Next, go to the Bank Cards section to add your international Visa or Mastercard credit card (Visa is more widely reliable) and complete the card issuer’s verification step.
2. **How to pay**
3. 
  - **Offline in stores**: Tap the “Pay” button on the homepage to display your payment QR code for the merchant to scan. You can also tap “Scan” to scan the merchant’s QR code and enter the amount yourself.
  - **Online purchases**: Select Alipay at checkout on supported platforms; you will be redirected to the Alipay app to confirm and complete the payment.

4. **Important notes** Always turn off VPN before making payments, as IP anomalies will trigger risk control blocks. Transactions under RMB 200 are typically exempt from the 3% foreign card transaction fee, so you can ask merchants to split larger bills to reduce extra costs. If you run into verification failures or payment errors, contact human customer support through the Help Center and submit your passport for manual review.

## How to Use WeChat Pay in China for Tourists

![How to Use WeChat Pay in China for Tourists](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/1n955324x99kwjokh8C94.jpg)

- **Pre-departure setup** Download the WeChat app, register an account with your mobile number, and complete SMS verification. Go to **Me > Services > Wallet > Bank Cards** to add your international Visa or Mastercard, and follow the prompts to complete identity verification (you may use your passport). Note that you may encounter more verification steps than with Alipay.
- **How to pay**
- 
  - **Offline in stores**: Access your payment code via **Services > Receive/Pay**, or use the main “Scan” function to scan merchant QR codes.
  - **In-mini-program payments**: Scan QR codes on restaurant tables, shop entrances, or service kiosks to open WeChat Mini Programs. You can order food, book tickets, or access services directly inside the mini-program and pay instantly via WeChat Pay.

- **Important notes** WeChat Pay has much stricter risk control. Frequently switching devices, locations, or using a VPN will very likely trigger account restrictions or daily spending limits (commonly RMB 2,000 per day for foreign users), and there is almost no way for short-term visitors to increase these limits or unlock accounts easily. Some merchant QR codes only work with mainland Chinese bank cards, so foreign cards may be declined without explanation.

## Payment Fails in China? What to Do if Alipay or WeChat Pay Is Not Working

![How to Diagnose Why WeChat Pay Is Not Working in China](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M70u12000tnb405y63AF.png)

![Payment Fails in China? What to Do if Alipay or WeChat Pay Is Not Working](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M75l12000tnb41lxC4F0.png)

When a payment fails at checkout, the issue usually stems from identifiable technical triggers. Below are the root problems paired directly with their actionable fixes.

1. **Disable VPN before paying**: Both payment systems flag VPN IPs as high risk. Turn off all VPN apps before making a payment. If you use a travel eSIM with built-in VPN routing, switch to local WiFi or disable the VPN feature for payment apps.
2. **Carry at least two different cards**: Prioritize a Visa card for best compatibility, and bring a second card (ideally Mastercard or another issuer) as a backup.
3. **Keep some RMB cash handy**: It is illegal for merchants in China to refuse cash. Having a small amount of cash on hand will cover emergencies, remote areas, or sudden account restrictions.
4. **Match names where possible**: Try to use a bank card where the name matches your passport name as closely as possible. Long names or multiple surnames can cause automatic verification failures.
5. **Do not share one card across multiple accounts**: Binding the same bank card to multiple Alipay/WeChat accounts will trigger risk controls and cause payments to fail.

## FAQs about Alipay vs WeChat Pay

- Should I use Alipay or WeChat Pay in China?

 For most international tourists, Alipay is the recommended first choice due to its dedicated Tourist Edition (Tour Pass) designed specifically for foreigners, straightforward international card linking, and a powerful mini-app ecosystem that includes Didi ride-hailing, Meituan food delivery, and China Rail booking — all without downloading extra apps. However, downloading both is strongly advised. WeChat Pay is essential for scanning restaurant menus, splitting bills with Chinese contacts, and day-to-day social payments. Together, they cover virtually every payment scenario you'll encounter in China.
- Can US citizens use WeChat Pay?

 Yes, US citizens can use WeChat Pay in China. You can register a WeChat account with a US phone number and link an international credit or debit card (Visa or Mastercard) to activate WeChat Pay. However, foreign-linked accounts may have lower transaction limits and a foreign card fee may apply. It's best to set up your account before arriving in China, as some verification steps are easier to complete from outside mainland China. Note that WeChat (and WeChat Pay) has faced regulatory scrutiny in the US, so check current App Store availability in your region before your trip.
- How can I avoid the 3% transaction fee on Alipay?

 Alipay charges a 3% fee on transactions above ¥200 when paying with a foreign-issued credit or debit card; transactions of ¥200 or less are free. To reduce or avoid this fee: (1) Split larger bills so each transaction stays under ¥200; (2) Pre-book major travel expenses — flights, hotels, and tours — through platforms like Trip.com that accept international cards directly, reducing the amount you pay through Alipay; (3) Check if your bank offers fee-free international transactions that offset the cost. (Please refer to Alipay's official website for the latest fee information.)
- What is the best app to pay in China as a tourist?

 Alipay is generally considered the best payment app for tourists visiting China. Its Tourist Edition (Tour Pass) was specifically designed for international visitors, allowing easy setup with a foreign passport and international credit card. Alipay's built-in mini-programs also let you book Didi taxis, order food via Meituan, and even purchase China rail tickets — all within one app. WeChat Pay is an excellent secondary option, especially for restaurant QR ordering and any social payments with Chinese locals. Having both apps installed covers virtually all payment scenarios during your China trip.
- What is the difference between Alipay and AlipayHK?

 Alipay and AlipayHK are two completely separate apps serving different regions. Alipay (支付宝) is designed for mainland China, operates in Chinese Yuan (CNY/RMB), and is developed by Ant Group under Alibaba. AlipayHK (支付宝HK) is a distinct app for Hong Kong, operates in Hong Kong Dollars (HKD), and is tailored to Hong Kong's regulatory and banking environment. If you're traveling to both mainland China and Hong Kong, you may need both apps. Tourists visiting only mainland China should download the standard Alipay app, while Hong Kong visitors should use AlipayHK.
- Do I need both Alipay and WeChat Pay, or is one enough?

 While Alipay alone can handle most payment needs for tourists in China, having both apps is strongly recommended by experienced travelers. WeChat Pay is deeply integrated into restaurant dining culture (scan-to-order menus), social bill-splitting, and communication with local contacts. Alipay excels at transport, e-commerce, and tourist services via its mini-programs. Think of it this way: Alipay is your financial utility tool, WeChat Pay is your social payment tool. Both are free to download, and the peace of mind of having both active is well worth the 10-minute setup time for each.
- Should I use Digital RMB (e-CNY) instead of Alipay or WeChat Pay?

 Digital RMB is useful as a zero-fee alternative if you find merchants that accept it, but its acceptance remains significantly narrower than Alipay and WeChat Pay as of 2026. Set it up as a supplementary option after establishing Alipay and WeChat, but do not rely on it as your primary payment method. Stick with Alipay as your main choice and WeChat as your backup.
- What is the best backup plan if my phone dies or my app crashes during a trip?

 Carry RMB cash for emergencies, keep your international Visa or Mastercard as a second backup for hotels and upscale venues, and store a screenshot of your Alipay and WeChat Pay QR codes in your phone's camera roll or cloud storage. If your phone battery dies, cash is the only option, which is why carrying at least 200 to 300 RMB is essential for peace of mind.
