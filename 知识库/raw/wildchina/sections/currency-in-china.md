# Currency in China

Currency in China is mostly digital now, with mobile payment apps like WeChat Pay and Alipay being widely used, though cash is still accepted. Here are some essential tips on currency exchange, using credit cards, and managing your expenses during your journey.

 electricity The RMB

Officially, China uses the Renminbi (RMB), which translates to the ‘People’s Currency.’ The currency is also known locally, and more commonly internationally, as the yuan (CNY). The official symbol for the yuan is ¥, but in most shops in China, you will see the Chinese character 元 used. Click here for a quick currency converter. Hong Kong uses the Hong Kong Dollar (HKD) and Macau uses the Pataca (officially, although HKDs are more commonly used).

 Cash (and QR codes) are king

Cash is the easiest way to pay for things in China as a visitor, unless you have WeChat Pay or Alipay. However, when you’re on the ground, you won’t see many locals using hard currency – people in China pay for nearly everything on their phones by scanning QR codes in apps like WeChat or Alipay. Non-Chinese bank cards can be linked to Alipay, so if you’re interested in a fully immersive experience, we suggest downloading the app and getting your card linked prior to your trip!

electricity Exchanging money and ATMs

The easiest way to exchange your money for Renminbi (RMB) or Hong Kong Dollars (HKD) is before you depart, at a bank in your home country. Many ATMs in Hong Kong and China accept foreign cards and you can withdraw local currency at a similar exchange rate to what you would find back home. In larger cities, changing money is convenient practically everywhere. In rural areas, it might be more difficult to find a place to change money. Wherever you are, your guide will always be able to help you find the nearest place to get local currency. We recommend double-checking with your credit card company to ensure that your card will work in ATMs in China, and that there are no surprise daily withdrawal limits. At the end of your trip, if you still have RMB left over, we recommend exchanging it before you leave or in the airport, as it can be hard to convert RMB back to USD or other currencies outside of China.

 Credit cards in China

In many major cities, you’ll have no significant problems using a credit card. You’ll usually be able to use your credit card at mid-sized to large hotels, restaurants, and retail chains. While most large restaurants accept credit cards, most small businesses do not.

Visa, Mastercard, American Express, and Discover are accepted in China, although to varying degrees. While credit cards tend to offer the best exchange rate, please note that companies vary widely in the amount of surcharge they may add to each transaction.

Outside of the major cities, China is largely a cash and QR-based economy. Because of this, you might have trouble finding ATMs and businesses that accept credit cards in rural areas.

 electricity So, how much cash will you need?

Generally, on a WildChina journey, you’ll only need money for personal expenses, meals on your own, wine, liquor, other beverages, and gratuities for your guides or drivers.

While travel habits vary, we’ve found that withdrawing around USD $100 per person per day is typically enough to cover most personal expenses and gifts.
