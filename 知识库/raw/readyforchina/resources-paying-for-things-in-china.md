# Paying for Things in China: A Guide for Foreigners

## Forget Everything You Know About Travel Payments

When most people travel abroad, they get some cash in the local currency and make sure their bank cards work internationally. But China is refreshingly different—and in many ways, much simpler.

You need exactly two things to pay for everything in China: **a payment app** (AliPay is best, as we'll explain) and **a data connection** (see our recommended eSIM below). With these two essentials, you can pay for everything from your taxi ride from the airport to your hotel, to a bottle of water at the Great Wall of China.

### Our Real Experience

Even small vendors in remote areas are generally more comfortable with an AliPay QR code than they would be with physical cash. In our last three business trips to China, we haven't even seen a single note of physical money.

Street food vendors, mountain-top souvenir shops, and rural bus drivers—they all prefer digital payments. It's that universal.

## Why China's Payment System is Different

### Your Credit Cards Won't Work

Most places in China simply don't accept international credit cards. Even if they have card readers, they're typically for Chinese bank cards only.

### Cash is Increasingly Rare

You'll struggle to pay for anything with cash. Many vendors don't even carry change, and some younger staff have never handled physical money.

### Digital is King

China is the world's most digitally advanced payment ecosystem, with adoption rates that dwarf other countries.

### Incredibly Convenient

Once set up, it's faster and easier than anywhere else in the world. No fumbling for change or waiting for card authorizations.

### How AliPay Works for Tourists

#### The QR Code System

Everything revolves around QR codes—those square barcodes you'll see everywhere. There are two ways to pay:

- **Scan their code:** Point your camera at the merchant's QR code
- **Show your code:** Display your payment barcode for them to scan

#### Why It's So Common

- • Instant transactions (2-3 seconds)
- • No cash handling or change needed
- • Automatic receipt and record keeping
- • Works for any amount (even 1 yuan purchases)
- • Reduces theft and counterfeiting risks

## Getting Started: The Step-by-Step Process

### Important: Set This Up Before You Travel

While it's possible to set up AliPay after arriving in China, it's much easier and less stressful to do it at home with a stable internet connection and familiar environment.

### Download AliPay

Get it from the official Apple App Store or Google Play Store. Make sure it's the genuine app by "Alipay (Hangzhou) Technology Co., Ltd."

### Create Your Account

Sign up with your international phone number and set the app language to English. You'll receive an SMS verification code.

Upload clear photos of your passport. This step is mandatory for foreign users and usually completes within a few hours.

### Add Your International Card

Link your Visa, Mastercard, or other international credit/debit card. AliPay will verify it with a small temporary charge.

## Real World Payment Scenarios

Here's what paying for things in China actually looks like with AliPay:

### Airport to Hotel

Taxi driver shows you their QR code. You scan it, enter the fare amount, and pay. No language barrier, no cash needed.

**Tip:** Screenshot your destination in Chinese to show the driver

### Street Food

Point at what you want, scan the vendor's QR code on their phone or printed sign, confirm the amount, and enjoy your meal.

**Tip:**Most vendors won't accept cash even if you offer it

### Shopping Malls

At checkout, show your AliPay barcode. The cashier scans it, payment processes automatically. Faster than card payments back home.

**Tip:** Have your payment code ready before reaching the cashier

### Public Transport

Generate a transit QR code in AliPay, scan it at metro gates. Works in major cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou.

**Tip:** Set up transit cards in the app before using

### DiDi Rides

Book through DiDi app (China's Uber) with AliPay as payment method. Rides automatically charge your linked card.

**Tip:** Much cheaper and more reliable than regular taxis

### Tourist Attractions

Many museums and scenic spots have QR codes for ticket purchases. Skip the lines and buy tickets instantly through AliPay.

**Tip:** Some attractions offer discounts for mobile payments

### Travel Tip: Must-Try Beijing Street Food

Skip the touristy Wangfujing street food and head to **Guijie (Ghost Street)** for authentic Beijing flavors. Try **jianbing** (Chinese crepes) for breakfast and **xiaolongbao** (soup dumplings) from local vendors.

**Payment Reality:**Most street food vendors here are QR-code only and won't accept cash. Having AliPay ready means you can be spontaneous and try anything that catches your eye without worrying about payment methods.
