## What TenPayGo Is

On **June 28, 2026**, Chinese financial outlet Jiemian reported that Tencent is internally testing a "daily-life convenience assistant for visitors to China" — a one-stop digital-life app whose centerpiece is mobile payments. The app had quietly appeared on the Apple App Store a week earlier, on **June 21**, under the name **TenPayGo** (one word — the spacing "TenPay Go" you see in some headlines isn't the official name).

The name is a hint at what's under the hood. **TenPay** (财付通) is Tencent's licensed payment company — the same entity behind WeChat Pay. TenPayGo is essentially a **separate, English-only front door** to that payment network, aimed at foreign visitors who just want to pay and don't want to set up the full WeChat super-app.

The core idea

You download one small English app, link a card, and pay at any merchant that accepts WeChat Pay — by showing a payment code or scanning the merchant's code. No WeChat account, no Chinese social app, no Chinese phone number implied by the design. If it delivers, it removes the most painful part of paying in China as a tourist: the setup.

## What's Confirmed

These facts come from the Apple App Store listing itself and Tencent-attributed reporting — they're solid:

It's genuinely from Tencent

The App Store developer is **Tencent Technology (Shenzhen) Company Limited** — the same developer account behind WeChat and QQ. It is not a copycat app.

Launched June 21, 2026 (iOS only)

It went live on the App Store on June 21 (version 1.0.1 followed on June 24). It's **iPhone-only, English-only**, requires iOS 15+, and there's **no Android version** on Google Play yet.

It pays at WeChat Pay merchants

Tencent's own description says that at merchants accepting Weixin (WeChat) Pay, you can pay directly with TenPayGo — riding on the existing acceptance network that covers millions of shops nationwide.

Both QR flows, funded by a linked card

Screenshots show both **"Show code to pay"** (you present a payment code) and **"Scan to pay"** (you scan the merchant's code), with spending drawn from a linked **Bank Card**, plus an in-app billing/history view.

Sign up with just an email — no Chinese phone, card, or real-name

Multiple outlets (TechNode, Bloomberg, PYMNTS) and Tencent's own framing agree: you register with **only an email address**. No mainland phone number, no domestic bank card, and no WeChat account — the exact barriers that make regular WeChat Pay hard for short-term visitors.

Funded by Visa, Mastercard, or Apple Pay

Reporting on the beta consistently lists **Visa, Mastercard, or Apple Pay** as the funding options you link. (Tencent's official rules page says the exact eligible card types are shown on the in-app service page, so treat other networks as tester reports — see below.)

Fee: free on ¥200 or less, then 3%

TenPay's official international-card rules state that transactions **of ¥200 or less are exempt from the international-card fee**, and amounts **above ¥200 are charged 3%** of the transaction amount (refunds reverse the fee proportionally). Some testers report being charged 3% regardless of size, so always trust the fee shown on your own payment screen.

Small-scale test, opening gradually

Tencent-attributed reporting and App Store reviews agree it's in a limited test ("Under Internal Testing"). It's on the store, and while many still can't finish sign-up, **some early testers are now getting in and paying** — it's no longer completely closed.

## What Early Testers Report

These come from early hands-on reports by people who got into the beta. They're useful and consistent, but **unofficial** — and because the app is clearly still being tuned, some of this may be A/B tested or change without notice. Treat it as "what people are seeing," not settled rules:

Gmail works; Outlook/iCloud may say "in testing"

Testers report signing up smoothly with a **Gmail** address, while some Outlook and iCloud addresses hit an "internal testing" message — suggesting the beta is rolled out by email domain, not officially documented.

Cards that bound successfully

One tester reports binding **Plasma One, an HSBC card, and N26**, and says the app also lists **American Express and JCB** alongside Visa/Mastercard. Amex/JCB aren't officially confirmed, so treat them as reported-not-guaranteed until you see them on your own screen. You can also skip binding a card and pay via a card already in**Apple Pay**.

Most personal & merchant QR codes work; some don't

Testers say it scans **most personal and business WeChat collection codes**, but **codes from third-party payment providers** (some point-of-sale code-plates) didn't work. Scanning your own code to pay yourself is a classic fraud-control red flag — don't do it, it can drag in the receiving account.

## What's Still Unknown

Some details travelers care about still have **no official answer**. We're not going to guess — treat anything you read about the following as unconfirmed until Tencent or the app itself states it:

The full list of eligible card networks

Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay are cited across coverage, and testers add Amex/JCB, but Tencent's official rules only say the eligible card types are shown on the in-app service page. Confirm on your own screen.

Spending limits & ID verification at full launch

Per-transaction or annual caps aren't published. And while the beta needs no real-name verification, that's widely expected to be a temporary beta perk — **passport verification is likely once it opens to everyone**.

Which countries it's really for

It's downloadable in many App Store regions, but the actual eligibility for the test (by country/account) hasn't been spelled out.

## Can You Actually Use It Today?

**Maybe.** It's opening up: some early testers (often with Gmail sign-ups) are now getting in, linking a card, and paying. But it's still a rough, gated beta, and many people hit a wall:

Sign-up is gated

Across multiple App Store regions, reviewers report the same thing: you can't get past the sign-up / verification step because the app is in internal testing. Early hands-on reports also describe crashes and rough edges — consistent with a pre-release build.

Low ratings = "launched but unusable"

Its early App Store ratings are low in several regions, but read the reviews: the complaints are mostly "why release it if I can't sign up," not problems with actual payments. It's a maturity issue, not necessarily a quality verdict.

If you're traveling to China *soon*, do not plan around TenPayGo. Set up a proven method now (see below) and treat TenPayGo as something to try later, once it's open.

## How It Differs From WeChat Pay & PayPal-Scan

TenPayGo is the latest of several ways Tencent is courting foreign spenders. Here's where it sits:

| Method | What you install | Status |
|---|---|---|
| TenPayGo | Standalone English app, link a card | Beta — some testers can pay |
| Foreign card on WeChat Pay | WeChat app + bind card | Live, years of use |
| PayPal × WeChat scan | PayPal app (no Chinese app) | Live, U.S. users first |
| Foreign card on Alipay | Alipay app + bind card | Live, widely used |

The key distinction: TenPayGo is a **dedicated app** for visitors, where [PayPal-scan](/blog/paypal-in-china-wechat-pay) lets you reuse an app you already have, and the classic route binds a card inside [WeChat Pay](/guides/using-wechat) or [Alipay](/guides/using-alipay). One early limitation analysts flag for any WeChat-rails method: a lot of Chinese daily life (ordering, tickets) runs inside **WeChat mini-programs**, which a standalone pay app may not cover. We'll test that claim when the app opens.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Is TenPayGo a real Tencent app?

Yes. On the App Store its developer is Tencent Technology (Shenzhen) Company Limited — the same account behind WeChat and QQ. It launched on June 21, 2026 and is currently in a small-scale internal test.

Can I use TenPayGo right now?

Maybe. It's a gated beta: some early testers (often with Gmail sign-ups) are getting in and paying, but many people still can't get past the sign-up/verification screen. If you're traveling soon, don't rely on it — set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign card as your main method instead.

Do I need WeChat to use TenPayGo?

No — that's the point. It's a standalone English app that pays at merchants accepting WeChat Pay, without installing WeChat itself. You link a bank card and pay by showing or scanning a QR code.

Which cards does TenPayGo accept, and what are the fees?

Coverage of the beta cites **Visa, Mastercard, or Apple Pay** as the funding options (early testers also report Amex and JCB, which aren't officially confirmed). On fees, TenPay's official international-card rules say transactions of **¥200 or less are free** of the international-card fee, and amounts **above ¥200 are charged 3%**. Some testers report being charged 3% regardless, so always check the fee on your own payment screen. Spending limits aren't published yet.

Is there an Android version of TenPayGo?

Not as of late June 2026. It's iPhone-only (iOS 15+) and isn't on Google Play.

TenPayGo vs. the PayPal × WeChat link — what's the difference?

TenPayGo is a new, dedicated app you install and link a card to. The [PayPal route](/blog/paypal-in-china-wechat-pay) lets U.S. PayPal users scan WeChat Pay codes from the PayPal app they already have. Both ride WeChat Pay's merchant network, but PayPal-scan is live today while TenPayGo is still in testing.

Heading to China before TenPayGo opens?

Don't wait on a beta. Set up a proven payment method now — Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign card covers virtually every payment scenario in China today.
