## The Universal Setup: Linking Any US Card to Alipay & WeChat Pay

Before we dive into bank specifics, the process of adding a card is the same for all of them. We highly recommend starting with [Alipay](/guides/using-alipay), as it's generally more reliable for tourists.

1. **Download & Register:** Get the Alipay app and sign up with your US (+1) phone number.
2. **Add Card:** Go to **Me > Bank Cards > + Add Card**.
3. **Critical Name Match:** Enter your name EXACTLY as it appears on your passport. First and Middle names go in the "Given Name" field; Last name goes in the "Surname" field.
4. **Passport Verification:** Follow the prompts to scan your passport's photo page to fully activate your account.

For a complete, screen-by-screen walkthrough of this process, check our foundational guides on [how to set up Alipay](/guides/using-alipay) and [WeChat Pay as a foreigner](/guides/using-wechat).

## Bank-Specific Analysis: How Does Your Card Stack Up?

Here's what you can expect from each of the major US banks based on real-world user reports and known behaviors.

### 1. Chase Bank Cards (Generally High Success Rate)![Chase Bank Logo](/images/banks/chase.svg)

Chase is often considered the most travel-friendly of the major US banks, and their cards tend to perform well when linked to Chinese payment apps.

| Popular Cards | Compatibility & Tips |
|---|---|
| **Chase Sapphire Preferred® & Reserve®** | **Excellent.** As premier travel cards, their fraud detection systems are often better attuned to international transactions. This is your best bet for a smooth experience. |
| **Chase Freedom & Slate** | **Good.** These cards generally link without issue. However, being non-travel-focused, they might be slightly more prone to a security decline on your first transaction. Be ready to approve it in your Chase app. |

**Chase-Specific Tip:** Chase no longer offers travel notices—their fraud detection is fully automatic. Before you leave, make sure your phone number and email are current in the Chase Mobile® app so you can respond quickly to any security alert. If a transaction does get flagged in China, you can approve it directly in the app or call the number on the back of your card to remove the hold.

### 2. Bank of America Cards (Good, but Can Be Cautious)![Bank of America Logo](/images/banks/boa.svg)

Bank of America cards work well, but their security systems can be notoriously cautious. Proactive communication is key.

| Popular Cards | Compatibility & Tips |
|---|---|
| **BoA Travel Rewards & Premium Rewards** | **Good.** These cards link successfully. However, BoA is known for declining the first one or two transactions abroad until you verify them. Don't panic when this happens. |
| **BoA Customized Cash Rewards** | **Fair to Good.** Success is high, but be prepared for the first transaction to be flagged. The best practice is to make a small, ¥1-2 purchase to trigger the security check, approve it, and then proceed with your real payment. |

**BoA-Specific Tip:** Bank of America no longer requires a formal travel notice—their system monitors for suspicious activity automatically. However, BoA's fraud detection remains aggressive on China transactions, so ensure your mobile number and email are current in the BofA app so you can approve any flagged transaction instantly. If you'd like extra peace of mind, calling the number on the back of your card before you fly is still a valid (if optional) step that may help you avoid a ["Transaction Declined by Issuer" error](/tool/specific-error/fix-issuer-declined-payment-error-C6-1011).

### 3. Citi Cards (Good Reliability)![Citi Bank Logo](/images/banks/citi.svg)

Citi cards are generally reliable for linking to Chinese payment apps, and their customer service is often helpful if you encounter issues.

| Popular Cards | Compatibility & Tips |
|---|---|
| **Citi Double Cash® & Premier®** | **Good.** These cards are reported to work well. Citi's system seems to handle the initial verification charge from Alipay/WeChat Pay smoothly. |
| **Costco Anywhere Visa® Card by Citi** | **Good.** Despite being a co-branded card, it operates on Citi's reliable network and generally links without problems. |

**Citi-Specific Tip:** Citi allows you to create virtual card numbers. **Do NOT use a virtual card number** to link to Alipay or WeChat Pay. The verification process requires a card that can be traced back to your real identity, and virtual numbers will fail.

## What Does It Cost? Fees & Transaction Limits

Before you arrive, it's worth knowing what each platform charges when you pay with a foreign card.

#### WeChat Pay — Fees & Limits for Foreign Cards (2026)

- **Transaction fee:** **3%** on any single payment over RMB 200 (~US$28). Payments under RMB 200 are completely fee-free.
- **New user bonus:** First-time international card users enjoy a **60-day fee waiver** on daily transactions up to RMB 1,000—ideal for short trips.
- **Single transaction cap:** RMB 6,000 (~US$836)
- **Monthly cap:** RMB 50,000 (~US$6,970)
- **Annual cap:** RMB 60,000 (~US$8,366)

Practical tip: During your 60-day new-user window, most tourist spending on WeChat Pay is free. After that window, consider using Alipay or a no-foreign-fee card like Wise for purchases over RMB 200.

#### Alipay — Fees for Foreign Cards (2026)

Alipay does **not** charge foreigners an additional transaction fee for card-linked payments. Your issuing bank may still apply its own foreign transaction fee (typically 1–3%), so choosing a no-foreign-fee card remains worthwhile.
