## 1. Apple Maps / Amap (Gaode Maps) - Your Go-To Navigation Tools

Navigation is make-or-break for travel in China, and these two apps eliminate the need for Mandarin skills, perfect for used to Google Maps.

### Apple Maps (iOS Only)

![China Transport Apps: Apple Maps](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M72g12000rmtkmnsC492.png)

The best choice for iPhone-users. It automatically switches to China’s Gaode Maps data when you’re in the country, keeping everything in English.

- Key Features: Accurate metro/bus routes, live traffic updates, offline map downloads (critical for low-data areas), and Pinyin search (e.g., “Tiananmen Square” or “Jingan Temple”).
- Pro Tip: Search for landmarks or addresses in English or Pinyin. Apple Maps will translate it for local navigation. Download offline maps for your destination city before leaving Singapore (Settings → Maps → Offline Maps).

### Amap (Gaode Maps) - Android Users’ Top Pick

![China Transport Apps: Amap](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M74612000jgdl4372850.png)

Alipay’s official map partner, [Amap](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/amap.html) got a full English update in 2025 and is the most accurate app for Android users. It’s the local’s go-to for a reason, with hyper-specific details like traffic light timers and real-time bus arrival times.

- Key Features: English interface, bike/scooter routes, taxi hailing, and integration with Alipay (pay for rides directly in the app).
- Pro Tip: Link your Alipay account (with your card) for one-tap payments for all Amap transport services. No extra steps needed.

## 3. Didi - Hail Taxis/Rideshare (No Language Barrier)

![China Transport Apps: Didi](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M75d12000rmtl424A03E.png)

Didi is China’s Uber, and booking it via Alipay means skipping the Didi app’s messy identity verification and linking directly to your Alipay walle. Drivers rarely speak English, but Didi (via Alipay) lets you pin your **exact** location for seamless pickups.

- Key Features: English in-app navigation for the driver, fixed fares (no surge pricing surprises), and payment deducted directly from your Alipay balance/linked card.
- How to Use:
- 
  1. Open Alipay → Tap the Transport icon → Select Didi.
  2. Enter your destination in English (Alipay translates it to Mandarin for the driver).
  3. Confirm your pickup spot (pin a landmark if needed) and tap “Book”—no extra app download required!

- Alternatives: If Didi is busy, use Amap Taxi (cheaper for short rides) or HelloRide. Both work with Alipay, and Google Lens translates any Chinese text on the screen instantly.

## 4. Alipay (Scan) - Unlock Shared Bikes (No Deposit, No Hassle)

![China Transport Apps: Alipay](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M73z12000rmtm96hB8B9.png)

Alipay is the best tool you need for shared bikes in China. It unlocks all major brands (Hellobike, Meituan) with a single scan, no deposit required, and fares (¥1.5–3/hour) are charged directly to your linked card.

- Key Features: Real-time map of nearby bikes, no Mandarin setup, and automatic payment via Alipay.
- Pro Tips:
- 
  - Always park bikes in painted yellow/white zones (you’ll be fined ¥5–20 for illegal parking. Alipay marks these zones on the map!).
  - Check the bike’s brakes and seat height before riding—some are well-worn, and Alipay lets you report faulty bikes for a refund.
  - Avoid Meituan/Didi branded bikes. They require their own apps; stick to Alipay-supported brands for simplicity.

## 5. Alipay Transport - The All-in-One QR Code for Metro/Bus

![China Transport Apps: Hellobike](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M70412000rmtm9zw72BC.png)

This is Alipay’s biggest win for travellers. A single QR code that works for metro, buses, and even ferries in every major Chinese city.

- How to Set Up:
- 
  1. Open Alipay → Tap the Transport icon (look for the train/bus symbol) on the homepage.
  2. Select your city (e.g., Shanghai) → Tap Agree and Obtain Card (one-time setup).
  3. Your QR code will appear. Scan it at metro turnstiles or bus scanners (same code for entry and exit!).

- Key Features: No top-up required (fares are charged per ride), English instructions, and the code works across multiple cities.
- Perk: Integrates with your existing Alipay setup if you already linked your card for payments, transport is ready to go.

## 6. MetroMan - Subway-Specific Navigation

![China Transport Apps: MetroMan](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M74b12000rmtkarkADB6.png)

If you’re sticking to China’s big cities and relying on the metro, MetroMan is a must-have. It’s an English-first app with offline subway maps for 50+ cities, breaking down every transfer, exit, and station layout.

- Key Features: Offline access (no internet needed), step-by-step transfer guides, and station amenities (ATMs, toilets, wheelchair access).
- Pro Tip: Pair it with Apple Maps/Amap for the last leg of your journey. MetroMan gets you to the right exit, and your navigation app takes you to your final destination.

## Pro Tips for Using China’s Transport Apps

![China Transport](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M73n12000rmtmky238A1_Q60.jpg)

### 1. Stay Connected (Critical for All Apps)

All transport apps need internet, and China blocks Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram. A China eSIM is the best option.

- Buy one at Changi Airport or via Trip.com. It lets you use all apps, receive SMS verifications for Alipay/WeChat Pay, and bypass VPN restrictions.
- Avoid physical SIM cards (they require a Chinese ID) — eSIMs are activated in 1 tap with your passport.

### 2. Link Your Payment Methods First

All top transport apps integrate with Alipay. WeChat Pay works too, but Alipay has better transport integration (one QR code for everything). Stick to Alipay for transport, and WeChat Pay for small daily purchases.

### 3. Download Offline Maps Before You Leave Singapore

The internet can be spotty in rural areas or on high-speed rail. Download offline maps for your destination cities on Apple Maps/Amap/MetroMan while you’re still in Singapore.

### 4. Pin Exact Locations

Drivers and transit apps rely on precise pinpoints. For pickups, pin a landmark instead of a street address.

### 5. Carry a Small Amount of Cash

While all apps are cashless, carry ¥100–¥200 CNY (≈SGD20–SGD40) for rural areas, tiny street stalls, or if your phone dies.

## FAQs About China Transport Apps

- Can I use these apps with my Singaporean mobile number (+65)?

 Yes! All top transport apps (Trip.com, Alipay, Amap) let you register and verify with your +65 number — just enable international roaming or a China eSIM to receive SMS codes. No Chinese phone number required.
- Do I need a Chinese bank account to use these apps?

 No. All apps accept Alipay/WeChat Pay with your linked card (Visa/Mastercard). This is the biggest win. No local bank account hassle.
- What’s the best app for high-speed rail bookings?

 Trip.com is the only app with full English support, e-tickets, and accepts all Singaporean bank cards. China’s local rail apps are Mandarin-only and require a Chinese ID, so Trip.com is the only viable option.
- Can I use Didi without Alipay/WeChat Pay?

 You can download the standalone Didi app and pay with an international card, but booking via Alipay is far easier. It skips Didi’s identity verification and links directly to your existing payment setup.
- Are shared bikes in China safe for travellers?

 Yes. Most bikes are well-maintained, and Alipay marks bike lanes and safe parking zones on the map. Just follow local traffic rules (ride on the right side of the road) and check the brakes before riding.
