## 1. Choose Where to Go

With our knowledge of China and feedback from our clients, the following five destinations are recommended for first-timers. **Each one brings something different**: ancient culture, stunning landscapes, city life, or even a close-up with China's beloved pandas.

![Top Cities to Visit for First Trip to China](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

### 1) Beijing — Explore China's Imperial Past

![Beijing, The Forbbiden City](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

If it's your first time in China, Beijing is the ideal starting point, where ancient dynasties, iconic landmarks, and vibrant local life all come together.

[The Great Wall](/greatwall/section/) and [Forbidden City](/beijing/forbidden-city/how-to-visit.htm) are must-sees. To make your visits a fresh twist, try visiting the Forbidden City in Hanfu. It's photogenic and offers a deeper cultural feel.

**For a new experience in the Great Wall**, you could expect a drone-delivered experience (a fun blend of tradition and tech) at Badaling. Or head to Jinshanling for a sunset picnic on a quieter, more scenic stretch.

You could also get a feel for daily life with [a guided hutong walk](/beijing/tours/hutong-tour.htm), where Beijing's old neighborhoods still breathe.

**If you travel with kids**, spend a day at Universal Beijing Resort, or enjoy some fun and educational activities like tai chi in the park, calligraphy, and a local family visit in the hutongs.

Beijing is also where you can **experience China's futuristic lifestyle**, from smart home demonstrations to a ride in an autonomous vehicle.

**A Chinese clinic experience** has become a fresh and popular highlight of a China trip. We'll take you to a local clinic to explore TCM pulse reading, facial diagnosis, and even AI-assisted analysis.

You can also try acupuncture, cupping, or tuina. It's available not only in Beijing, but also in Xi'an and Hangzhou.

### 2) Shanghai — China's Window to the Future

![The Bund, Shanghai](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

Shanghai is where China looks ahead. It is a city of dazzling skylines, global flavors, and vibrant contrasts between the old and the new.

**Hop on a ferry** to cross the Huangpu River. You will see the historical architecture on the Bund as well as the modern skyscrapers opposite, giving you a distinctive contrast between modern life and the post-imperial past.

We have specially designed an in-depth Bund culture discovery walking tour for those who want to learn more interesting stories about the Bund and old Shanghai.

You will **walk a route that few tourists will find** by themselves, see the most original bricks, and hear some interesting stories that you'll never find in history books.

To feel the city's softer side, we recommend walking through the tree-lined streets of the French Concession, where Art Deco villas, boutique cafés, and local life blend seamlessly.

If time permits, extend your tour to Hangzhou or Suzhou for a blend of classical gardens, water towns, and modern high-tech experiences. See our [5-Day Shanghai and Hangzhou High-Tech Tour](/china-tours/high-tech-tour.htm).

### 3) Xi'an — Walk Through 2,000 Years of History

![Xi'an, The Terracotta Warriors](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

If you're drawn to China's ancient roots, Xi'an is unmissable. As the capital of 13 dynasties and the starting point of the Silk Road, it offers a deep dive into the foundations of Chinese civilization.

[The Terracotta Warriors](/xian/terracotta-army/how-to-visit.htm) are the city's most iconic highlight, but **we can take your visit a step further**. With access to a lesser-known museum near the site, you'll get a rare close-up of the first batch of restored warriors.

Step into a real conservation lab, where under the guidance of professional restorers, you'll work with authentic Qin-era fragments and try your hand at piecing together a mini terracotta warrior.

![Lintong Museum](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

In the evening, don't miss **Great Tang All Day Mall**. It is a dazzling boulevard lit by lanterns and full of performers, street artists, and music. It's the perfect spot to experience Xi'an's modern pulse wrapped in Tang Dynasty charm.

Xi'an is also a favorite for Hanfu photography lovers. The city's many historic buildings and atmospheric streets make the perfect backdrop. We can help you dress up and capture it all, from palace walls to quiet temples.

### 4) Guilin — Cruise Through China's Most Iconic Scenery

![Guilin Li River](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

First-timers love Guilin for its beautiful landscapes and relaxing places to escape from the cities. It is a true masterpiece of classic rural China.

**A Li River cruise** is the best way to enjoy karst peaks, combined with the Li River.

Yangshuo and Longsheng, two counties next to the city of Guilin, offer most of Guilin's top highlights.

[Yangshuo](/yangshuo/) is a place where children can put down their phones and iPads and still be well entertained… by cycling around the idyllic countryside, exploring an undeveloped karst cave, and even experiencing the life and work of a Chinese farmer.

Yangshuo also offers a natural, romantic atmosphere for couples. A romantic riverside candlelit dinner set in a mountain retreat garden could be a great way to celebrate your 20th, 30th, 40th, etc., anniversary. Contact us to design a special time for you.

If you are interested in minority culture, the Longji Rice Terraces in Longsheng are really a highlight, both for enjoying the magnificent tiered fields and for experiencing the world of the Zhuang and Yao minorities.

### 5) Chengdu — Get Close to Pandas

![Chengdu,the hometown of giant pandas](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

If pandas are on your must-see list (and for most visitors, they are), Chengdu is the place to go. But there's much more to this laid-back city than adorable black-and-white bears.

An even more special experience would be to have a close encounter with pandas by taking part in our giant panda volunteer program — cleaning the glass of their enclosures, making panda food, watching a panda eat it, and more.

**Beyond pandas**, Chengdu is all about slowing down and savoring daily life. Sip tea in a centuries-old teahouse while locals play mahjong, explore food through street markets, or enjoy a private face-changing opera encounter, where you can meet the performers and even learn a few tricks.

You could also easily extend your trip to [Jiuzhaigou National Park](/jiuzhaigou/) to enjoy its fairyland-like turquoise lakes, colorful forests, and dramatic alpine scenery.

## 3. Consider When to Travel to China

![When to Travel to China](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

The "best time" really depends on what you're hoping to experience.

If you're coming for iconic sights like the Great Wall, the Forbidden City, or the Terracotta Warriors, **spring and autumn** are generally your safest bet.

Between April and May or September and October, the weather is pleasant, the skies are clear, and everything just feels more photogenic.

Fall is usually a bit drier and warmer than spring, which makes it slightly more comfortable for long walks and outdoor activities.

**A more ideal travel time** for you could be March and early April or early November if you'd prefer to skip the crowds and don't mind slightly cooler mornings or evenings. There are fewer people, lower prices, and still nice weather for sightseeing.

And while summer might sound intense. But it's not as tough as you might think. Rain tends to come in short bursts rather than all-day downpours, and many cooler regions like Yunnan or Tibet are at their best this time of year.

With a bit of smart planning (and air-conditioning), summer in China can actually be very doable, especially for families on school holidays.

Winter, on the other hand, is quiet and budget-friendly. You could enjoy the Ice and Snow Festival in Harbin or wander through Beijing's palaces without the usual crowds. If you don't mind bundling up, it's a great time to get a more peaceful look at China.

You can find more detailed tips on the [Best Times to Travel to China](/weather/china-best-times.htm).

## 4. What's the Best Way to Travel around China

Traveling within China might seem daunting at first, as the distances are big, and the cities are vast. While it is not difficult to travel around China because China has one of the most advanced, affordable, and reliable transport networks in the world.

For long distances, **high-speed trains** are often the best choice. They're clean, efficient, and remarkably fast. Trains are also a great way to see more of the real China beyond the cities from the scenery outside the window.

If you're short on time or heading to places that are farther apart (like Beijing to Guilin), domestic flights can be a smart option. Major airports are well-connected, and there are multiple flights per day for most routes. We'll help you choose the best flight times to fit your itinerary smoothly.

**Within cities**, getting around is easier than you might expect. Most major cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Chengdu have modern subway systems with English signage and announcements. Taxis and ride-hailing apps (like DiDi, China's version of Uber) are widely available.

If you prefer a more relaxed experience, choose a private driver and guide. This can make all the difference, especially if you're traveling with kids, seniors, or just want the convenience of door-to-door service without the stress.

We'll help you choose the right mix of transport for your journey — fast trains, smart flights, easy local rides — all arranged in a way that feels seamless and lets you focus on enjoying the trip.

## 5. Choosing Where to Stay

China has no shortage of accommodation, from luxury international chains to boutique courtyard hotels and family-run guesthouses. But finding one that fits your needs, comfort level, and travel style can make a big difference to your experience.

If you're looking for comfort and ease, **international brands** like Hyatt, Marriott, Hilton, or InterContinental are widely available in major cities.

Staff at these hotels usually speak English, and you'll have access to familiar services and amenities. They are perfect if it's your first time in China or you're traveling with kids.

If you want something with more character, **boutique hotels**, especially in places like Beijing's hutongs, Guilin's countryside, or Lijiang's old town, can give you a unique cultural experience.

You might even wake up in a traditional courtyard with carved wooden beams.

We help match you with the right place based on your preferences, whether that's being close to attractions or having special requests (like a crib, connecting rooms, or early check-in).

**One note**: not all hotels in China are licensed to host foreign visitors, especially in smaller towns. But don't worry — we only recommend places that meet international standards and ensure a smooth check-in process for foreign travelers.

## 6. Do I Need a Visa for China?

Traveling to China is now much easier for most international travelers — **visas are not necessary**.

If you're from most of Europe (like Italy, Spain, Germany, France, Belgium, etc), Southeast Asia (like Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, etc.), Australia, or New Zealand, you can now enter China visa-free for up to 30 days.

If you're from the U.S., Canada, the U.K., or other 52 countries, there's still a great option: China currently allows [10-day visa-free](/travelguide/visa-application/10-day-visa-free.htm) transit in most cities like Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, Zhangjiajie, Chengdu, and Guangzhou.

If you want to stay longer than 10 days, you'll need a tourist (L) visa or an even easier option: let us help you apply for a China Port Visa, which doesn't require a visit to the embassy or consulate.

## 7. What Does a China Trip Really Cost?

Traveling in China is more affordable than many people expect, especially once you're on the ground.

The biggest cost for most travelers is the international airfare, which can vary a lot depending on season and departure city. For example, flights from the US or Europe usually range from $1,200 to $3,000 USD for an economy round trip.

Once you're in China, the cost of a private, comfortable trip is quite reasonable. For a mid- to high-quality private tour including 4–5 star hotels, domestic transport (flights or high-speed trains), attractions, private guide, transfers, and lunch, you can expect to spend around **$220–350 USD** per day per person.

**Timing matters:**

Hotel and flight prices can increase by 50–100% during China's peak travel times, especially the first weeks of May and October (known as the Golden Weeks), summer vacation, and around Christmas and New Year. Planning your trip outside these windows can help you save significantly.

Traveling in spring (March–April) or late autumn (November) can enjoy better prices, pleasant weather, and smaller crowds.

## 8. Learn How to Pay in China: Go Cashless with Ease

China is one of the most cashless countries in the world. In big cities and even small towns, mobile payment is the norm: people pay for everything from metro rides to street food using their phones.

If you're wondering, "[Can foreigners use mobile payment in China now](/expatslife/payment-methods.htm)?" The answer is yes. It's now easier than ever.

You can simply link your international credit card (Visa/Mastercard) to popular apps like WeChat Pay or Alipay. Once set up, you can scan and pay just as locals do — no need to worry about exchanging cash or carrying coins.

WeChat will also be your main channel to communicate with your local tour guide.

**Our advice**: We recommend linking your cards and completing the setup while you are still in your home country, as you may need to receive a verification code during the process. Once in China, some overseas phone numbers may have trouble receiving these codes, which can make the setup more difficult.

Other options:

- **Cash is still accepted**, especially in more traditional places, though it's not always convenient to find change.
- **International credit cards are accepted** in large hotels and some high-end restaurants or stores, but not in many local spots, small vendors, or public transport.

## 9. Prepare Internet Access in China

**China's internet environment is unique**: many popular global websites and apps, including Google, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and YouTube, are blocked in China.

The easiest option is to arrange your mobile data before you leave. **You can either purchase an international data plan from your local mobile provider or buy an [eSIM](https://esim.holafly.com/)** that supports access to international services in China.

Both options give you mobile data as soon as you arrive in China, while also allowing you to access services such as Google, WhatsApp, and Facebook without setting up a separate VPN. This is one of the easiest ways to stay connected throughout your trip.

Alternatively, you can set up a reliable [VPN](https://www.chinahighlights.com/havealook/bestworld.htm) before you arrive. A VPN can help you access websites and apps that are blocked in China.

## 10. Should I Choose Guided Tours?

![A must-see city in your first china trip: Guilin Longji](https://data.chinahighlights.com/grey.gif)

Traveling independently in China is possible — but it's not always easy, especially if it's your first time.

Language barriers, complex transport systems, and the sheer size of the country can make even simple things feel overwhelming. Booking tickets, navigating train stations, or finding the right entrance to a massive site like the Forbidden City can eat up precious travel time and energy.

That's where guided tours come in.

**A well-planned private tour can remove the guesswork and stress.** You'll have a local expert who speaks your language, handles logistics, and shares fascinating stories that bring the sites to life — things you might miss on your own.

And unlike traditional group tours, private tours are flexible. You decide the pace, the interests, the food, and the vibe. Want to explore a quiet part of the Great Wall with a picnic? Done. Prefer hands-on experiences like making dumplings with a local family? Easy.

If you're traveling with kids or elderly family members, or just want to see more in less time, having someone local to guide you makes all the difference.
