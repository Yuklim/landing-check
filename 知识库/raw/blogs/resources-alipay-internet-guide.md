# Alipay & Internet in China 2026 — Step-by-Step

## 1
Part 1: Alipay 2026 — Foreign Card Setup, Fees & Limits

China is 95% cashless. Street vendors use QR codes. If Alipay isn't working when you land, you can't buy a bottle of water. Set this up **before your flight.**

### Step-by-Step: Alipay with Foreign Visa/Mastercard

Download the **International Version** of Alipay

App Store / Google Play. Search "Alipay" — blue icon. Do NOT download the China-only version (支付宝中国版). The international version supports English natively.

Register with your **home country phone number**

US, UK, EU, AU numbers all work. You'll receive an SMS verification code. **Keep this number active** — if you log out inside China without international roaming, you're locked out.

Complete **Real-Name Verification** with your passport

Path: Me → Settings → Account & Security → Identity Verification. Take a clear photo of your passport ID page. **Critical:** enter your name exactly as it appears on the MRZ (machine-readable zone) at the bottom of your passport — surname first, then given names. A mismatch = verification rejected.

Face scan

Alipay will prompt you to take a live selfie. Good lighting, no sunglasses. Takes 10 seconds.

Add your Visa / Mastercard

Path: Me → Bank Cards → Add Card. Enter card number, expiry, CVV. Alipay supports Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB, Discover, and Diners Club. **Pro tip:** add TWO cards (e.g., Visa + Mastercard) so you have a backup if one gets blocked.

Set a 6-digit payment PIN

You'll use this for every transaction. Pick something memorable — you'll type it dozens of times a day.

**Test it before you fly**

After verification, try topping up ¥10 on your own phone number via Alipay. If the transaction goes through, you're ready. If not, debug at home — not on a street corner in Shanghai.

### 2026 Fee Structure — Know Before You Swipe

| Transaction Size | Alipay Fee | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **≤ ¥200** (~$28 USD) | 0% — FREE | Buying a coffee, metro ride, street snack |
| **> ¥200** | 3% | ¥300 → ¥9 fee; ¥500 → ¥15 fee |

💡 Split-payment trick:

For purchases >¥200, politely ask "Can I pay in two transactions?" (in Chinese: 中文 可以分两次付吗？ 拼音 + 图标 (边框 pill) Kěyǐ fēn liǎng cì fù ma? ![🔊](/_astro/volume-notice.CPIaiMcZ.svg) 无拼音时: 仅图标 (也有边框) ). Splitting a ¥350 bill into ¥175 + ¥175 keeps both under ¥200 and avoids the 3% fee entirely. Most vendors happily oblige.

**Additional costs to watch for:** Your home bank may charge a foreign transaction fee (1–3%). Use a **no-FTF credit card** (Chase Sapphire, Capital One, Revolut, Wise, Monzo). The exchange rate is set by Visa/Mastercard at the interbank rate — Alipay adds no markup.

### Transaction Limits (Verified Passport Users)

| Limit Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Single transaction | $5,000 USD (~¥35,000) |
| Annual cumulative | $50,000 USD (~¥350,000) |

⚠️ Without passport verification: $500 single / $2,000 annual. Complete verification or you'll hit the wall on day 3.

### What Foreign-Linked Alipay *Cannot* Do

- **Personal transfers (P2P):** Can't send money to individuals. Street vendors using personal QR codes = foreign cards blocked. Use cash for these.
- **Red packets (红包):** Can't send or receive — this is a WeChat-centric feature anyway.
- **Some mini-programs:** Certain in-app services (utility bills, some ticket bookings) require a Chinese ID card.
- **WeChat Pay is worse:** WeChat Pay's foreign card setup is less reliable. Many foreign cards get rejected during verification. Use Alipay as primary; WeChat Pay as backup communication app only.

## 2
Part 2: The Airport SIM Trap — Why You Shouldn't Wait

The most common mistake first-time visitors make: "I'll just buy a SIM card at the airport." Here's why that's a terrible plan.

### The Airport SIM Death Spiral

**1. Real-name registration queue.** Chinese law requires all SIM cards to be registered to a real identity with a passport scan. At PVG (Shanghai Pudong), the airport carrier kiosks routinely have **45–90 minute queues**. You've just stepped off a 12-hour flight and now you're standing in line.

**2. The airport Wi-Fi requires a Chinese phone number.** To get online and use the airport's free Wi-Fi, you need to receive an SMS verification code — to a Chinese phone number you don't have yet. It's a catch-22: you need internet to get a SIM, you need a SIM to get internet.

**3. Your home SIM's roaming may not work.** Many US/EU carriers have expensive or unreliable roaming in China. Verizon's TravelPass is $10/day. T-Mobile's free roaming throttles to 128kbps (unusable for maps or payments).

**4. Without internet, Alipay may not load.** Alipay requires an active data connection to process payments. If you land, can't get online, and your VPN doesn't auto-connect — **you're stranded.** No Didi, no metro QR code, no translation app, no map.

**5. Airport carrier kiosks sell only Chinese-language plans.** The staff may not speak English. The plan you buy may have restrictions (no international calls, data caps) you won't understand.

Real Reddit report, r/chinatravel (2025):

"Landed at PVG at 6pm. SIM card counter had 30+ people queuing. Airport Wi-Fi demanded a Chinese number for the SMS code. My VPN wasn't connecting on airport Wi-Fi. Spent 90 minutes unable to do anything. Had to beg a stranger to hotspot me so I could call a DiDi to my hotel. Do not do what I did."

## 3
Part 3: The Solution — eSIM Before You Fly + Backup VPN

The optimal setup, tested and confirmed by thousands of travelers on r/chinatravel: activate an international eSIM before departure, and install a VPN as backup for when you're on hotel Wi-Fi.

### Best eSIMs for China (2026)

International eSIMs work by **routing your mobile data through servers in Hong Kong, Singapore, or your home country**. Because your traffic never enters China's domestic internet infrastructure, the Great Firewall does not apply — Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Gmail all work on mobile data without a VPN. This is *not* a VPN feature; it's a consequence of how international data roaming is architected.

**⚠️ Important:** This only covers **mobile data**. Hotel WiFi, café WiFi, and airport WiFi are Chinese domestic networks — they're behind the firewall regardless of which eSIM you use. You still need a VPN for those (see below).

### Why You Still Need a VPN — Even with an eSIM

Your eSIM's international data routing only applies to **mobile data**. The moment you connect to **hotel Wi-Fi, café Wi-Fi, or airport Wi-Fi**, your traffic travels through China's domestic internet — and you're back inside the Great Firewall. A VPN encrypts your traffic and tunnels it out of the country, restoring access to Google, WhatsApp, and Gmail on any network. Think of it as: eSIM = your cellular data is always free; VPN = your safety net for WiFi and backup if mobile data drops.

Quick FAQ

### "Can I use Apple Pay / Google Pay in China?"

No. Apple Pay and Google Pay have near-zero merchant acceptance in China. The two dominant platforms are Alipay and WeChat Pay. Alipay is the one you want.

### "Do I really need cash? I thought China was cashless."

Yes, carry ¥500–1,000. It's legally required to be accepted everywhere, and it's your fallback when a street vendor's QR code doesn't work with foreign-linked Alipay. ATMs at Bank of China and ICBC accept foreign cards reliably.

### "What if my Alipay stops working mid-trip?"

Most common cause: your bank flagged a transaction as fraud. Call your bank (use your eSIM data). Second cause: Alipay triggered re-verification. Use the in-app support chat (English available). In the meantime, use cash or your backup card.
