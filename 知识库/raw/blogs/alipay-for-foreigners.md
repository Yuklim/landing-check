### Using Alipay in China || How to Top-up Alipay?

When spending money as a foreigner using Alipay you have two options:

#### 1 – Link Your Bank Card to Alipay

This is quick and easy to set up (with less caveats) but is not guaranteed to work.

Linking a bank card in Alipay is easy. Just follow these steps:

- Open Alipay
- Click the **ME** icon on the bottom right of the screen
- Click **BANK CARDS**
- Click the **+ icon** on the top right of the screen
- Enter your bank card number, or scan your bank card

You’ll see the list of bank cards that can be linked with Alipay. These currently include Visa, Mastercard, Unionpay, and Diners Club.

> **NOTE ||**There is no American Express functionality but we believe they are looking to add this in the near future.

![](https://ltl-school.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/link-bank-alipay-480x398.jpg)

![](https://ltl-school.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/link-banks-alipay-480x436.jpg)

Now, the important thing to note with this method is that it may or may not work.

**Unfortunately there is no way of knowing straight away.**Just because Alipay says its supports Visa cards, it doesn’t mean your transaction will go through in China.

So it’s important to have a back-up… hence the 2nd method…

#### 2 – Open a Tourcard

This method is more foolproof because what you’re essentially doing is opening a bank account through Alipay, and it takes just a few clicks.

![Alipay for Foreigners || How to use Tourcard](https://ltl-school.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/alipay-link-card-e1688361797235-242x480.jpg)

There are a few downsides though, so let’s run you through it.

- Open Alipay and search **TOURCARD**
- **Open the Tourcard** mini-app (Alipay includes hundreds of mini-apps you can find by searching)
- Click **ENTER**
- Click **USE NOW** and verify your personal info
- **Choose an Amount**to Top Up
- **Make your payment**

In completing these steps you’ve effectively opened up a brand new **BANK OF SHANGHAI** account in a matter of minutes.

Pretty cool, right?!

You won’t get a bank card though, this is simply an account you will use inside of Alipay.

### Can US Citizens (and Other Foreigners) Use Alipay?

**Yes.**Since 2023, Alipay has opened its platform to international visitors, including US citizens, EU passport holders, UK citizens, Australians, and most other nationalities.

**You don’t need a Chinese bank account or a Chinese phone number to get started.**

What you do need:

- A valid international passport for identity verification
- A supported foreign bank card (Visa or Mastercard)
- A working international phone number to receive a verification SMS

The setup process takes around 10 minutes inside the app. Once verified, you can pay at millions of merchants across China.

### Alipay Identity Verification for Foreigners

Alipay requires all foreign users to verify their identity before making payments. Here’s what to expect:

1. Open Alipay and go to **Me > Settings > Identity Verification**
2. Select **Non-Mainland China Resident**
3. Choose your document type: **Passport** is the standard option for most visitors
4. Enter your passport number and full name exactly as it appears on your passport
5. Take a clear photo of your passport data page when prompted
6. Complete the facial recognition scan (live selfie)
7. Wait for approval, this is usually instant but can take up to 24 hours

> **TOP TIP💡** Do this before you land in China, ideally at home with a stable internet connection. Some users report issues completing verification on Chinese mobile networks for the first time.

### Alipay Fees for Foreigners || What You Need to Know

When you link a foreign Visa or Mastercard to Alipay, a **3% currency conversion fee** applies to each transaction. That adds up fast if you’re spending daily in China.

**TIP💡 How to avoid the 3% fee:** Top up your Alipay TourCard using a card with zero foreign transaction fees (such as a Wise card or a Starling/Chase card in GBP/USD).

When you fund the TourCard you convert once at a competitive rate, and your in-China spending via the TourCard itself carries no per-transaction surcharge.

Quick fee overview:

| Payment method | Fee to note |
|---|---|
| Foreign Visa/Mastercard linked directly | ~3% per transaction |
| Alipay TourCard (topped up via Wise) | Wise mid-market rate on top-up only; 0% in-app |
| Chinese bank account linked | 0% |

**Spending limits also apply to TourCard:**check the current limits inside the TourCard mini-app, as they are updated periodically.

### Is Alipay Safe for Foreigners?

**Yes, Alipay is a legitimate, regulated payment platform operated by Ant Group, which is supervised by China’s central bank (People’s Bank of China).**

It uses 256-bit SSL encryption and has a built-in escrow system (originally designed for Taobao/Tmall purchases) that holds funds until a transaction is confirmed.

For foreigners, a few practical safety tips:

- **Set a payment passcode** immediately after account setup, you’ll be prompted to do this.
- **Enable face or fingerprint authentication** inside the app settings.
- Link only one card initially and monitor your first few transactions.
- If a payment looks suspicious, Alipay has a 24/7 dispute resolution centre accessible through the app’s Help section.

Millions of international visitors use Alipay safely every year. The main risk is losing your unlocked phone, so treat it like your wallet.

### Does Alipay Work Outside China?

Alipay itself is primarily designed for use within mainland China.

However, **Alipay+**, the international cross-border payment service, is accepted at merchants in:

- **Southeast Asia**: Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia
- **East Asia**: Hong Kong, Macau, Japan, South Korea
- **Europe**: Select merchants in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Netherlands, UK
- **North America**: Limited acceptance at selected retailers

If you’re studying at one of LTL’s schools in Singapore, Tokyo, Seoul, Hanoi or Saigon, it’s worth checking whether your Alipay account works locally, in many cases it does via Alipay+ partner networks.

> **TIP💡** Look for the Alipay+ logo at checkout rather than the standard Alipay logo when you’re outside mainland China.
