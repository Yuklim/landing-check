# Staying Connected in China (Internet)

Whether you want to keep in touch with your guide, share your experiences with family and friends back home, or connect with new acquaintances you meet along the way, having reliable internet access is important. To make the most of your time in China, we highly recommend you download WeChat on your phone.

 free_text Using eSIMs or SIM Cards in China

eSIMs (Recommended)

For most travelers, an eSIM is the simplest and most convenient option. It provides fast domestic data for maps, messaging, and translation apps — and, importantly, eSIM connections route through international gateways, so you can access most websites and apps without a VPN.

How to get one: Check your phone’s settings to confirm eSIM compatibility. Purchase and activate an eSIM online through providers such as Nomad before you arrive, or directly in the Alipay app by searching for “eSIM.”

WildChina tip: When purchasing through Nomad, use the promo code WILDCHINA at checkout to receive 150 Nomad Points (redeemable as a cash discount on a future Nomad purchase).

Best for: Travelers who need reliable data but don’t require a Chinese phone number.

SIM Cards

If your device does not support eSIM technology, prepaid SIM cards are available in China as an alternative to international roaming. To purchase a SIM card in China, you must present your passport, as regulations require phone numbers to be linked to IDs. You can also buy a SIM card in your home country before arrival, but you will need to provide your passport details during the purchase.

Two of the most convenient options for travelers are:

Roamingman: Located just after customs at Shanghai Pudong Airport. Offers short-term SIM cards with data and local calls. Registration is quick with your passport. Note: internet will be subject to China’s firewall, so install a VPN beforehand if you want to access restricted apps or sites.

China Telecom: A major Chinese carrier with a counter after luggage pick-up at Pudong Airport. Offers 7-, 15-, and 30-day SIM packages including data and domestic calls. Passport required for purchase; a VPN should be installed before arrival.

Using a VPN in China

In addition to having internet access through a SIM card or eSIM, you may want to access international websites and apps that are restricted in China. In this case, using a VPN (Virtual Private Network) is an option to consider. A VPN connects you to an international server, allowing you to bypass these restrictions. We recommend either ExpressVPN or Astrill. It’s important to download and set up your VPN before arriving in China, as access to VPN websites is blocked once you’re there.

International Roaming

Another way to access restricted sites in China is by using international roaming with your current mobile provider. When you roam internationally, a local Chinese carrier connects your device to your home carrier’s network, allowing your internet traffic to pass through as if you were still in your own country. This means you’ll be able to access the same websites and services, bypassing local restrictions. However, be aware that international roaming can incur significant charges, so it’s worth checking your provider’s rates before using this option.
