Tencent has launched TenPayGo, a standalone payment app for foreign visitors to China that works wherever WeChat Pay is accepted, and the most revealing thing about it is what it leaves out. Travellers sign up with an email address rather than a WeChat account, link Apple Pay, an international card or one of nearly 60 supported foreign e-wallets, and pay by scanning the same QR codes that Chinese users scan,[GSMArena reports](https://www.gsmarena.com/wechat_launches_a_new_payment_app_for_tourists_in_china-news-74760.php). WeChat’s strength in China has always come from tying payments to a person’s identity, phone number and social network, and TenPayGo is Tencent’s admission that, for tourists, those same ties have been the main obstacle.

## **What TenPayGo Does**

Tencent unveiled the app on September 24 at an international payments event in Shenzhen. It supports overseas bank cards from seven major card organisations, including Visa and Mastercard, along with Apple Pay and foreign e-wallets such as Starryblu, and once a payment method is linked, users can scan and pay directly through the app,[according to Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260924/8b5752e44dd84852a21fdfc39885b2f6/c.html). Every purchase is charged to the linked card or wallet, so visitors do not need to top up a separate balance or open a Chinese bank account.

Payments are only part of the pitch. The app can identify landmarks and objects from a photo and translate restaurant menus, and it will soon handle public transport tickets and departure tax refunds, starting in Shenzhen. Xinhua adds that visitors will be able to activate a ride code for Shenzhen’s buses and metro with no phone number or transit card required, and that Tencent plans to work with the clearing house NetsUnion to connect more overseas e-wallets to its network.

## **The Numbers Behind the Launch**

Tencent is building for a market that is growing quickly. According to Weixin Pay, the value and number of overseas e-wallet transactions in China rose 80.6% and 81.8% year on year in the first eight months of 2026, while official figures show foreign nationals made nearly 61.29 million border crossings in the same period, up 19.5%, with visa-free entries accounting for 23.72 million of them, up 26.8%. The timing also matters, because Tencent released the app ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen,[the South China Morning Post notes](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3368544/tencent-rolls-out-payment-app-foreign-travellers-ahead-apec-summit), when the city will host an unusually large number of international delegates who will all need to pay for taxis, meals and hotels.

## **Why a Separate App Instead of Fixing WeChat**

Foreign visitors could already use WeChat Pay before TenPayGo existed, since WeChat has allowed international cards to be linked to its wallet, but only from inside a WeChat account. Setting up that account is where many travellers get stuck, because the process is built around a verified phone number and identity checks designed for Chinese users, and a traveller who cannot complete them has no wallet at all. Alipay has the same structure, with guides for visitors listing a phone number and passport verification as the first steps before a card can be added,[China Travel Radar explains](https://chinatravelradar.com/en/pay/alipay-for-foreigners/).

TenPayGo removes that layer instead of trying to simplify it. By letting tourists register with an email address and pay through a wallet they already trust at home, Tencent is separating the payment rail, which foreigners need, from the super-app around it, which they mostly do not. The ride code that works without a phone number is the clearest sign of that thinking, because Chinese transit systems have usually assumed a local phone number sits behind every account.

There is also a competitive reason. Foreign wallets that plug into TenPayGo can reach the WeChat Pay merchant network without their users ever creating a WeChat account, which positions Tencent as the rail that overseas wallets connect to rather than a second app visitors are asked to install. With nearly 60 wallets already supported and NetsUnion working on more, the contest with Alibaba is shifting from who has the better tourist app to whose network foreign money flows through.

## **TenPayGo vs WeChat Pay vs Alipay: Which Should Tourists Use?**

For now, the answer is to set up more than one option, because each path has different requirements and TenPayGo is new enough that its limits are not yet clear.

|  | **TenPayGo** | **WeChat Pay (inside WeChat)** | **Alipay** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sign-up | Email address | WeChat account with phone number | Phone number and passport verification |
| Payment sources | International cards, Apple Pay, nearly 60 foreign e-wallets | Linked international cards | Linked international cards; some home wallets via Alipay+ |
| Where it works | Wherever WeChat Pay is accepted | WeChat Pay merchants | Alipay merchants |
| Extra travel tools | Photo recognition, menu translation, Shenzhen transit and tax refunds coming | Messaging, mini-programs | Mini-programs, transport and bookings |
| Fees on foreign cards | Not yet published | Commonly reported 3% above RMB 200 | Commonly reported 3% above RMB 200 |

The biggest open question is cost. Visitor guides commonly describe a 3% service fee on single foreign-card payments above RMB 200 on Alipay, with regulatory limits on foreign-linked cards applying to both Alipay and WeChat Pay,[China Neighbor notes](https://chinaneighbor.com/guide/essentials/alipay-for-foreigners-guide-2026/), but the launch coverage does not say whether TenPayGo follows the same rule or whether a linked e-wallet changes the fee. A traveller who mostly buys coffee and metro rides will barely notice the difference, while one paying for hotels and train tickets through the app should check the fee screen before confirming.

The practical advice is therefore to install TenPayGo for everyday spending if your home wallet or card is supported, keep Alipay as a backup for merchants or services that do not accept WeChat Pay, and carry a small amount of cash for the rare shop that accepts neither.

## **What Tourists Give Up for the Convenience**

A standalone app that handles payments, transport, tax refunds and photo recognition becomes a detailed record of where a visitor goes, what they buy and what they point their camera at, even though it never asks for a phone number. Readers of GSMArena’s report raised the same concern in the comments, and it is a reasonable question to put to Tencent, since the launch materials describe the features in detail but say little about how long visitor data is kept or who can access it. None of that is unusual for a payment app, and card networks at home collect similar spending data, but travellers who care about privacy should read TenPayGo’s data policy before linking their main card, and consider linking a separate card or e-wallet used only for the trip.

## **FAQs**

### **What is TenPayGo?**

TenPayGo is a payment app from Tencent’s Weixin Pay for foreign visitors to China. It lets travellers pay wherever WeChat Pay is accepted using international cards, Apple Pay or supported foreign e-wallets.

### **Do I need a WeChat account to use TenPayGo?**

No. TenPayGo is a separate app, and you sign up with an email address rather than a WeChat account.

### **Which payment methods work with TenPayGo?**

It supports cards from seven international card organisations including Visa and Mastercard, Apple Pay, and nearly 60 overseas e-wallets.

### **Does TenPayGo charge fees?**

Tencent has not published TenPayGo’s fee structure in its launch materials. Alipay and WeChat Pay commonly charge 3% on foreign-card payments above RMB 200, so check the app’s fee screen before paying.

### **Can I use TenPayGo for public transport in China?**

Shenzhen is first. Tencent says visitors will be able to activate a ride code for Shenzhen’s buses and metro without a phone number or transit card.
