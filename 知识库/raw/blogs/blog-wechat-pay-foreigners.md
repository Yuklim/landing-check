# WeChat Pay for Foreigners 2026: Setup, Amex, Fees & Limits

## Key facts (September 2026)

- **Cards accepted:** Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB, Diners Club and UnionPay.
- **Fees:** free for single payments of ¥200 or less; 3% above ¥200. New users get 60 days fee-free on daily spending under ¥1,000.
- **Limits:** ¥6,500 per transaction and ¥50,000 per month per WeChat's help center; regulatory ceiling US$5,000 per transaction.
- **No Chinese bank account or SIM needed.** Set it up at home before you fly.
- **Backup:** set up Alipay too, and carry a little cash.

Mobile payment is how China pays: by the end of 2023 its penetration reached 86%, the highest in the world. If you can't scan a QR code, ordering street food or paying a taxi gets hard fast. For years this was a nightmare for foreign tourists, because WeChat Pay and Alipay only worked with Chinese bank accounts. That has changed. Both apps now let you link an international card directly. Here's exactly how to set it up before you fly.

## Setup: 6 Steps, About 10 Minutes

#### Download WeChat

App Store (iOS) or Google Play (Android). Free. Create an account using your home phone number. You'll get a verification SMS.

#### Open the Wallet

Open WeChat → tap **Me** (bottom right) → **Services** → **Wallet**. If you don't see Services, update the app; some new accounts need a day or two before the payment feature appears.

#### Add your international card

Tap **Cards** → **Add Card** → **International Card** → enter your card number, expiry and CVV. Supported brands include Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB, Diners Club and UnionPay.

#### Set a 6-digit payment password

This is your PIN for every transaction. Choose something memorable but not obvious.

#### Verify your passport (recommended)

Upload your passport information page when prompted. Verification unlocks higher limits than an unverified account.

#### Pay by scanning

At any merchant, open WeChat → tap **+** (top right) → **Scan**. Point at the merchant's QR code, enter the amount and your PIN. Done.

**Updated September 2026: American Express now works.** An earlier version of this guide said Amex was not supported. A Beijing government guide published on March 26, 2026 lists American Express among the card brands visitors can link to WeChat Pay. Card-network support doesn't mean every card or payment will be approved, so we recommend bringing a second card on a different network.

**Do this BEFORE you fly to China.** Registration and card binding send SMS codes to your home number, which is easiest while you're still at home. New WeChat accounts sometimes need 24–48 hours before the payment feature activates.

## WeChat Pay Fees for Foreign Cards

**Payments of ¥200 or less are free. Above ¥200, WeChat Pay charges a 3% service fee** on the transaction. If you get a refund, the fee is refunded in proportion.

**New fee waivers (announced March 2026):** first-time international card users get the 3% fee waived on daily transactions under ¥1,000 for 60 consecutive days from their first payment, saving up to ¥30 per transaction. All international card users pay no fee on any single transaction under ¥200. Promotions can change, so check the offer shown in your wallet.

**Watch your own bank's fee too.** Your card issuer may add its own foreign transaction fee or exchange-rate markup. A card with no foreign transaction fee saves you money on every payment.

## Transaction Limits

You'll see two different sets of numbers online, and both are official. They measure different things:

| Source | Per Transaction | Monthly | Yearly |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeChat help center (what you'll usually see) | ¥6,500 (~$900) | ¥50,000 (~$7,000) | ¥65,000 (~$9,100) |
| National regulatory ceiling for visitors | US$5,000 | — | US$50,000 |

The regulatory ceiling is the maximum platforms are allowed to offer, not a promise for every account. Your usable limit depends on passport verification, your bank, the merchant and risk controls. For everyday spending (a $3 bowl of noodles, a $5 taxi, a $15 museum ticket) you'll never come close. For hotels, high-speed train tickets or big shopping, check the limit displayed in your wallet first, or pay by card at the hotel front desk or through Trip.com.

## WeChat Pay vs Alipay: Which to Use?

#### WeChat Pay

Also your messaging app. Chat with hotels, guides and drivers, join groups, translate menus. The all-in-one choice.

#### Alipay

English interface and direct card binding (the old Tour Pass prepaid card has been replaced). Great for transit and as a backup.

**Our recommendation:** set up both. Use WeChat Pay as your primary, because you'll need WeChat for messaging anyway, and keep Alipay as backup. Between the two, plus a little cash, you'll never be stuck.

## What You Can Pay For

- **Restaurants** — every restaurant has a QR code, even street stalls
- **Taxis and DiDi** — pay the driver by scanning, or through the DiDi mini-program inside WeChat
- **Street food vendors** — yes, the $0.50 dumpling lady has a QR code
- **Convenience stores** — 7-Eleven, FamilyMart and Chinese chains
- **Metro and bus** — some cities accept WeChat Pay directly; others need a transit mini-program
- **Hotels** — most accept WeChat Pay at the front desk
- **Museums and attractions** — book tickets through mini-programs
- **Supermarkets and wet markets** — scan at self-checkout or the vendor's QR
- **Hospital registration** — many hospitals use WeChat for booking and payment
- **Meituan food delivery** — order restaurant food to your hotel room via mini-program

## Troubleshooting

- **"Payment feature not available"** — new accounts can need 24–48 hours. Also try updating the app.
- **"Card declined"** — call your bank and tell them you're traveling to China; some banks block Chinese transactions by default. If one card keeps failing, try a card on a different network.
- **Amex card won't bind** — Amex is a supported brand, but some individual cards are still declined by the issuer or risk checks. Add a Visa or Mastercard as backup.
- **"Exceeded limit"** — complete passport verification, split the payment if the merchant agrees, or pay by card at the counter.
- **"QR code not scanning"** — use WeChat's own scanner (tap + → Scan), not your camera app.
- **"Cannot receive SMS"** — codes go to the number you registered with. Set up at home, before you fly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does WeChat Pay accept American Express?

Yes. A Beijing government guide published on March 26, 2026 lists American Express among the international card brands foreign visitors can link to WeChat Pay, alongside Visa, Mastercard, Discover, JCB, Diners Club and UnionPay. Network support does not guarantee every card or payment will be approved, so bring a second card on a different network.

### How much does WeChat Pay charge foreigners?

WeChat Pay charges no fee on single payments of ¥200 or less made with a linked international card, and a 3% fee on the full amount above ¥200. As of March 2026, first-time card users also get the 3% fee waived on daily spending under ¥1,000 for 60 days, saving up to ¥30 per transaction. Your own bank may add a foreign transaction fee.

### What are the WeChat Pay limits for foreign cards?

WeChat's help center lists ¥6,500 per transaction, ¥50,000 per month and ¥65,000 per year for international cards. Chinese regulators set a higher ceiling of US$5,000 per transaction and US$50,000 per year for foreign visitors. Your actual limit depends on verification, your bank and risk controls, so check the limit shown in your wallet before a large purchase.

### Do I need a Chinese bank account or Chinese phone number?

No. You can register WeChat with your home phone number and link a foreign card directly. Set it up before you travel, because the SMS verification code is sent to the phone number you register with.

### Is Alipay or WeChat Pay better for tourists?

Set up both. WeChat Pay is convenient because you already use WeChat to message hotels, guides and drivers, and Alipay is a strong backup with an English interface. Alipay's old Tour Pass prepaid card has been replaced by direct card binding, just like WeChat Pay.

### Should I still carry cash in China?

Yes, a small amount. Mobile payment is the norm, but Chinese authorities require merchants to accept cash, and cash is a useful fallback if a card is declined or your phone battery dies.
