## Best China Apps for Foreign Tourists

If you are planning a trip to China, your smartphone is your most important travel companion. The digital ecosystem in China is vastly different from the West, with many global giants like Google, Uber, and WhatsApp being unavailable or restricted. To navigate the country smoothly, you need to rely on a specific set of local applications. Before diving into the specifics, here is a quick snapshot of the must-have apps you should download before you fly.

## China Map Apps

![Local Map Apps in China: Baidu & Gaode](https://dimg04.c-ctrip.com/images/0M70i12000ihdxob5D2DF_R5.png_.webp)

![Local Map Apps in China: Baidu & Gaode](https://dimg04.c-ctrip.com/images/0M72312000ihdt20v182D_R5.png_.webp)

Forget Google Maps; in China, it is often outdated and lacks accurate public transport data. You need a local alternative to navigate complex subway systems and find your way around.

- **Amap (高德地图)**: The most popular map app in China. It offers precise real-time traffic updates, detailed subway exit guides, and even allows you to call a taxi directly within the app. It has a decent English interface for basic searches.
- **Baidu Maps (百度地图)**: A strong alternative to Amap with similar features. Some users find its English search function slightly more intuitive for specific landmark names.

## China Food Apps

![China Food Delivery Apps - How to Use Meituan/Eleme](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M72j12000jj202a3EC61.png?proc=format/f_webp)

Finding the best local restaurants and ordering food delivery is a huge part of the Chinese travel experience.

- **Dianping (大众点评)**: Essentially China's Yelp. It provides honest user reviews, star ratings, and photos of dishes. It is the best tool for finding highly-rated restaurants and avoiding tourist traps.
- **Meituan (美团)**: The go-to app for food delivery. If you are too tired to go out after a long day of sightseeing, you can order almost anything to your hotel. It also offers great deals on local attraction tickets and massage services.

## China Payment Apps: Alipay vs WeChat

![How to Pay by Payment Apps in China? How to Pay by Alipay](https://dimg04.c-ctrip.com/images/0M74d12000dguupxl2773_R5.png_.webp)

![How to Pay by Payment Apps in China? How to Pay by  Wechat Pay](https://dimg04.c-ctrip.com/images/0M73a12000dgv2lfjA6A9_R5.png_.webp)

Cash is rarely used in China, and credit cards are not accepted at many small vendors. You must rely on mobile payment apps.

- **Alipay**: Generally considered more foreigner-friendly. The interface is easier to navigate, and its customer service is more responsive if you run into issues. It allows you to link international credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, etc.) directly.
- **WeChat Pay**: Integrated into the WeChat messaging app. While slightly trickier to set up for foreigners, it is essential because some smaller vendors or specific mini-programs may only accept WeChat.
- **The Verdict**: **Install both.** Link your international card to both apps before you arrive. Most transactions under 200 RMB are free of service fees, while transactions over 200 RMB typically incur a 3% fee.

## China Taxi and Transport Apps

![What China Taxi/Ride Hailing Apps Work Best for Foreigners](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M77312000ly3chso4A19.jpg?proc=format/f_webp)

Getting around China is incredibly efficient, but you need the right tools to book rides and tickets.

- **DiDi (滴滴出行)**: China's version of Uber. It has an English interface and allows you to input your destination in text, so you don't need to communicate verbally with the driver. Payment is automatic through the app.
- **Trip.com**: The international version of Ctrip. It is the best all-in-one app for booking hotels, flights, and intercity trains. The customer service is excellent, and it supports passport registration and international credit cards.
- **12306**: The official China Railway app. While it offers the most accurate train schedules and no booking fees, the interface can be confusing for foreigners and may require a Chinese phone number for verification. For a smoother experience, use Trip.com to book your high-speed rail tickets.

Language barriers can be challenging, but translation apps are a lifesaver.

- **Google Translate**: Download the Chinese language pack for offline use. Its camera feature is fantastic for translating menus and street signs in real-time.

## China Social Apps

![China App Guide: RedNote](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M71z12000mi9vmkl6B9F.jpg)

While WeChat is the primary messaging tool, there are other social platforms you should know about to get the best local tips.

- **WeChat (微信)**: More than just a messaging app, it is a "super app" used for everything from messaging friends to ordering food via integrated Mini Programs.
- **Xiaohongshu (Red / Little Red Book)**: China's equivalent of Instagram combined with Pinterest. It is the absolute best platform for discovering trendy local cafes, hidden travel spots, and authentic itinerary recommendations shared by travelers.

## China Shopping Apps

![China App Guide: Taobao](https://dimg04.tripcdn.com/images/0M74n12000mi9ibgkF939_Q60.jpg)

Credit: from Vulcan Post | Use Taobao for online shopping in China

China's e-commerce ecosystem is incredibly advanced, offering everything from daily essentials to unique local crafts.

- **Taobao (淘宝)**: The largest online shopping platform in China. It offers an endless variety of products, from fashion to electronics. While the interface is primarily in Chinese, you can use your browser's translation feature or screen translators to navigate.
- **JD.com (京东)**: Known for its authentic products and lightning-fast logistics, especially for electronics and appliances. Delivery is often same-day or next-day in major cities.
- **Pinduoduo (拼多多)**: Famous for its group-buying model and extreme affordability. It is a great place to find cheap daily necessities and household items.
- **AliExpress**: If you are outside China but want to buy Chinese goods, AliExpress is the international version of Taobao. For those inside China, local platforms like Taobao and JD offer vastly superior delivery speeds and product variety.

## China Safety and Travel Utility Apps

These apps ensure your trip is safe, smooth, and hassle-free.

- **Umetrip (航旅纵横)**: The ultimate app for air travelers in China. It provides real-time flight status, gate change alerts, and aircraft history tracking.
- **110 / 120 Emergency Numbers**: Save these essential contact numbers in your phone: **110** for police emergencies and **120** for medical emergencies.
- **Alipay (Citizen Center / 市民中心)**: Located directly within the Alipay app, this hub lets you view city services, locate nearby hospitals, or double-check visa guidelines.

## FAQs about China Apps

- What app is mostly used in China?

 WeChat (微信) is the most widely used app in China, with over 1 billion active users. It covers messaging, video calls, cashless payments via WeChat Pay, restaurant ordering, taxi booking via DiDi, and much more — all within a single app. For Indian travellers, WeChat is the single most important app to download before visiting China.
- Which Chinese app is most popular for payments?

 Both WeChat Pay and Alipay (支付宝) are equally popular for cashless payments in China. For Indian travellers, Alipay is often recommended as the first payment app to set up because it has a full English interface and does not require an invitation to register — simply link your Indian Visa or Mastercard card before departure.
- What are the top 10 apps in China every traveller needs?

 The top 10 China apps for travellers are: 1) WeChat – messaging and payments; 2) Alipay – cashless payments; 3) DiDi – ride-hailing; 4) Baidu Maps or Amap – navigation; 5) Dianping – restaurant discovery; 6) Meituan – food delivery; 7) Xiaohongshu – local spot discovery; 8) Baidu Translate or Waygo – translation; 9) 12306 – train ticket booking; 10) A VPN or China eSIM – to access blocked international apps.
- Do Indian travellers need a VPN to use apps in China?

 Yes, a VPN is essential if you want to use apps blocked by China's Great Firewall — including WhatsApp (popular in India), Instagram, Gmail, Google Maps, and YouTube. Crucially, your VPN must be downloaded and activated before you land in China, as VPN websites are blocked within the country. A China eSIM is a convenient alternative that provides access to local Chinese apps and can bypass some restrictions.
- Can Indian tourists link their debit or credit card to WeChat Pay and Alipay?

 Yes. Both WeChat Pay and Alipay now allow foreign tourists, including Indians, to link an international Visa or Mastercard card directly — without needing a Chinese bank account. Alipay's registration is simpler as it does not require an invitation. Ensure you link your card and verify your identity with your passport before departing for China.
- Does Google Maps work in China?

 Google Maps is blocked in China and even with a VPN, its map data for China is often inaccurate or outdated. Indian travellers should download Baidu Maps (百度地图) or Amap (高德地图/Gaode Maps) before departure. Amap is particularly recommended as it has an English version and provides reliable walking, transit, and driving navigation across all major Chinese cities.
- Which apps should Indian travellers download before flying to China?

 Download all apps before boarding your flight, as access to Google Play Store and Apple App Store can be unreliable in China. The essential pre-departure checklist includes: a VPN (activate before landing); WeChat (register and find a contact to verify your account); Alipay (link your Visa/Mastercard); Baidu Maps or Amap; DiDi; Dianping; Meituan; Baidu Translate (download offline Chinese language pack); and 12306 if you plan to travel by high-speed train.
- Is Xiaohongshu (RedNote) available for Indian users?

 Yes, Xiaohongshu (also known as RedNote or Little Red Book) is available for download in India on both the Google Play Store and Apple App Store. It gained huge popularity in India in early 2025 when many Indian users joined following social media disruptions. The app supports English searches and has a growing community of expats and foreign travellers sharing China travel tips, making it a useful discovery tool for Indians planning a China trip.
