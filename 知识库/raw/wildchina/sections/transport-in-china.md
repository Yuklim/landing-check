# Transport in China

Whether you’re speeding across the country on a high-speed train, navigating bustling city traffic, or catching a domestic flight, here’s what you need to know about transportation in China.

A core part of WildChina’s mission is to break down barriers to China travel, whether by providing essential entry information, providing local guides to bridge language and cultural gaps, sharing expert advice for exploring remote regions, or making China physically accessible for all, from the Great Wall to the streets of Dali.

For a detailed overview of accessible travel in China, including tips and advice, check out our guide to accessible travel in China.

 electricity Bullet trains

China’s extensive train network is both modern and efficient, having expanded rapidly in recent years. Trains run with remarkable punctuality, often to the minute. Compared to flights, train travel in China offers a more relaxed experience with less-stressful security checks, more legroom, and access to phones and laptops. It’s also the most comfortable and scenic way to explore the country. And with speeds exceeding 300 km per hour, traveling on a bullet train is an experience in itself.

Seat assignment

Unlike flight tickets, which can be purchased well in advance with the option to choose your seat, train tickets in China typically become available only 15 days before departure. Seat selection is not an option when buying train tickets, as the Chinese train system automatically assigns seating. Securing tickets for business class, first class, or on popular train routes can be particularly challenging due to limited availability.

While WildChina will make every effort to accommodate your seating preferences during the reservation process, please be aware that these preferences cannot be guaranteed.

Security

As with airplanes, firearms, explosives, knives, toxins, corrosives, or other dangerous things are prohibited on the train.

Nail polish, nail polish remover, or hair dye are allowed, but no more than 50 ml (in total). Aerosols, such as deodorant, hair mousse, bug spray, and air refreshener, should be under 150 ml each and 600 ml in total. Two safety matches and two regular lighters are permitted per person. Alcoholic drinks (between 24% and 70%) are also okay, as long as they are fully sealed, with clear labels, and no more than 3000 ml.

 electricity Road conditions in China

In most areas, roads are well-maintained and developed, though they may not always meet the standards of Western countries. Rural and mountain roads can be bumpy and winding, so if you are prone to car sickness, it is advisable to bring motion sickness medication.

Traffic jams in China’s larger cities can be quite intense, with pedestrians, cyclists, cars, buses, and cabs all vying for space on the streets. If you find yourself stuck in one of these jams, the best approach is to sit back, relax, and take in Chinese urban life.

 Air travel

If your flight is delayed

While China’s train system is known for its punctuality, flights can experience delays, just like anywhere else. If your flight is delayed or canceled, you can reach out to your guide and trip organizer, who will handle the necessary arrangements to keep your trip on track. Whether it’s finding a quicker flight or switching to train travel, we will be working behind the scenes to ensure a smooth transition. If you need to leave the airport for a hotel or train, we will arrange for someone to accompany you.

Luggage restrictions

Most domestic airlines in China allow you one free checked bag (20kg, 44lbs) and one piece of carry-on (5kg, 11lbs). Please double check with your airline beforehand, as weight restrictions and allowances can vary between different carriers. Chinese airport security is thorough, and we recommend getting to the airport earlier than usual to ensure you have enough time to pass through security checks.

There are a number of restrictions on items you can pack in either checked or carry-on luggage listed below, but please check the regulations of your particular airline as well.

Lighters & Matches: Lighters and matches are prohibited on all Chinese flights, including in checked baggage.

Power Banks: Power banks must be placed in your carry-on luggage. As of June 28, 2025, Chinese airports no longer allow power banks to be brought through security unless they have clear 3C certification (see here). This rule applies to all domestic flights, including those departing from Hong Kong SAR, but it does not affect high-speed trains or direct international flights leaving China. As most foreign power banks do not have a 3C certification label, we recommend leaving your power bank at home and instead renting or purchasing one once in China. 

Carry-on Liquids: Liquids in carry-on luggage cannot exceed 100ml in volume and must be placed in a separate bag for inspection, similar to most airports.

Alcohol: You cannot carry alcohol in your carry-on luggage, even if the container is less than 100ml. Checked alcohol must be lower than 70% proof and limited to less than 5L in volume.

Prohibited Items: Other dangerous items, such as flammable, corrosive, poisonous, or radioactive substances, as well as firearms or lethal weapons, are strictly prohibited.
